\section{Introduction}
Multi-object tracking from an unmanned aerial vehicle is an observation problem
as much as an association problem. Density, target scale, occlusion, camera
motion, illumination, and blur change within a sequence, so a tracker receives
an observation stream whose quality and cost are both time-varying
\cite{zhu2022visdrone,du2018uavdt}. A fixed confidence floor can remove weak
but useful boxes in a sparse scene and retain clutter in a dense one; NMS can
merge nearby targets; and fixed resolution spends the same detector budget on
easy and difficult frames. These choices affect FP, FN, IDS, HOTA, MOTA, and
throughput together.

AC--MOT was introduced as a training-free runtime layer that computes cheap
scene cues, forms a Scene Complexity Index (SCI), and changes detector
confidence, NMS, and input resolution while keeping detector and tracker
separate. Its scientific value is the control interface: the detector
operating point becomes an auditable runtime variable rather than an immutable
deployment setting.

This paper follows the evolution from original AC--MOT through component
ablations, validation-calibrated profiles V1 and V2, and the stricter modern
policy frozen as v3. Historical evidence asks whether detector-side controls
change tracking behaviour and whether distinct calibrated profiles transfer.
The modern protocol asks whether a frozen operating point policy improves a
declared high-resolution baseline, whether the direction survives a second
tracker host, what quality--throughput trade-off is measured on a Tesla T4, and
whether exact scene-conditioned timing adds evidence beyond a useful mixture of
operating points.

The contributions are bounded and evidence-oriented:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We reconstruct original AC--MOT, including its five cues, historical SCI,
      temporal smoothing, Smart Calibrator, and three detector-side controls.
\item We document the V1/V2 evolution as distinct quality, identity, and runtime
      profiles, preserving the component trend and historical trade-offs.
\item We specify frozen v3 at implementation level: the crowd-selected SCI,
      surrounding state logic, thresholds, recovery probe, hysteresis, dwell,
      causal information flow, and action map.
\item We evaluate ByteTrack and a controlled OATrack host with aggregate metrics
      and paired 5000-resample sequence bootstrap intervals.
\item We measure end-to-end Tesla T4 latency and report detector, tracker, and
      controller components without calling a sub-30-FPS system 30-FPS real
      time.
\item We report no-retuning UAVDT transfer and matched-static and shuffled-
      timing controls to separate baseline-relative operating-point evidence
      from an unproven timing-specific causal benefit.
\end{enumerate}

The supported claim is precise. AC--MOT is an auditable training-free mechanism
for shaping the detector observation stream and computational operating point
of aerial MOT. The frozen evidence supports positive changes relative to the
declared baseline and strong ByteTrack transfer, with a smaller mixed effect on
OATrack. It does not establish universal improvement, official leaderboard
equivalence, or that exact scene-conditioned timing is necessary for the
observed accuracy gains. Historical and modern protocols are therefore kept
separate throughout the paper; extended historical and audit material is
provided in Supplementary Information.
