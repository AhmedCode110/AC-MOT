\section{Conclusion}
This paper followed AC--MOT as a scientific evolution rather than as one isolated controller. Original AC--MOT introduced a five-cue SCI, temporal smoothing, and a Smart Calibrator that adapted detector confidence, NMS, and resolution without retraining. Historical component ablations showed that these detector-side choices changed downstream tracking. V1 then provided a validation-calibrated quality profile, while V2 explored a deliberate identity, false-positive, and runtime trade-off. Historical VisDrone and UAVDT results demonstrate why the profiles must be treated as distinct operating points rather than collapsed into one universal winner.

The modern frozen v3 policy tightened the protocol. It selected a crowd-weighted SCI on a calibration split, retained explicit causal state machinery with recovery, hysteresis, and dwell logic, and mapped LOW, MEDIUM, and HIGH states to three frozen detector actions. Two tracker hosts, paired bootstrap intervals, T4 timing, and no-retuning UAVDT transfer made the quality--compute interaction measurable. The modern results support positive changes over the declared \texttt{r1536\_n70} baseline on both tracker hosts and a strong ByteTrack transfer, with mixed OATrack transfer.

The final scientific conclusion is narrower than a claim that switching always helps. Matched-static results are close to v3 and shuffled timing is at least as good on calibration, so the current evidence does not establish that exact scene-conditioned switching timing is necessary for the observed accuracy gains. It supports training-free detector operating-point control as a useful mechanism for shaping tracking quality and compute, while leaving causal timing benefit, universal tracker independence, and broader detector generalization for explicitly designed future experiments.
