\section{Frozen UAVDT transfer}
The modern transfer applies the frozen v3 policy to UAVDT without retuning thresholds, weights, action mapping, or tracker configuration. It contains 20 sequences and 16,592 frames, with 49,776 detector forward passes from the three action-specific cache pairs. This is a transfer test, not a new search: each host is run once and no bootstrap or cross-validation is applied.

For ByteTrack, the baseline has HOTA 45.923, MOTA $-1.573$, IDF1 58.400, IDS 601, FP 279616, and FN 66050. V3 has HOTA 47.004, MOTA 16.028, IDF1 61.767, IDS 486, FP 212077, and FN 73702. The changes are HOTA +1.081, MOTA +17.601, IDF1 +3.367, IDS -115, and FP -67539. The large false-positive reduction accompanies a false-negative increase of 7652. The transfer therefore supports a strong MOTA/precision direction for ByteTrack and is consistent with the selective operating-point interpretation; it is not a claim of an uncertainty-certified universal improvement.

For OATrack, the baseline has HOTA 47.728, MOTA 19.607, IDF1 62.101, IDS 517, and FP 197937. V3 has HOTA 47.640, MOTA 22.873, IDF1 62.931, IDS 486, and FP 183669. MOTA, IDF1, IDS, and FP improve, but HOTA changes by -0.087. This mixed result is scientifically important: a controller can improve some operational metrics while leaving the balanced HOTA measure flat or slightly worse. It also shows why transfer should be reported per tracker host rather than summarized as universal positive generalization.

The UAVDT result is bounded in three additional ways. The adapter and class-agnostic scorer are part of the frozen protocol, so it is not an official UAVDT leaderboard claim. The transfer is single-run per host and has no paired bootstrap interval. Finally, the detector was trained on VisDrone-DET, not on UAVDT, so the result tests frozen operating-policy transfer rather than detector-domain adaptation. Within those boundaries, ByteTrack transfer is a useful external stress test and OATrack transfer is evidence of tracker-dependent behaviour.

The contrast between the two hosts is especially useful for interpreting HOTA. ByteTrack's HOTA increases by 1.081 while its MOTA increases by 17.601, a pattern consistent with a large reduction in false positives and a simultaneous false-negative cost. OATrack's MOTA increases by 3.267 and IDF1 by 0.831, but HOTA decreases by 0.087. Thus MOTA recovery on a new dataset cannot be used as a synonym for balanced tracking improvement. The transfer section is retained precisely because it prevents a single favorable tracker result from being presented as dataset- and tracker-independent evidence.
