\section{AC--MOT evolution}
The historical component ablation is the first evidence that the original controller changed the tracking problem in measurable ways. The static component configuration (OLD--A0) obtained MOTA 17.633, HOTA 29.837, IDF1 30.892, and 283 IDS. Tuning ByteTrack alone raised HOTA to 30.864. Adding adaptive confidence and NMS produced HOTA 31.384, while adaptive resolution alone reached 32.446. The full historical combination (OLD--A3) reached MOTA 18.165, HOTA 33.064, IDF1 36.296, and 271 IDS. The progression does not prove that resolution is universally dominant, but it shows why a detector-side operating point deserves independent study: changes made before association altered both detection quality and identity outcomes.

Resolution is particularly informative because it changes the evidence available for small objects and the cost paid for every detector call. Confidence and NMS can alter the number and overlap structure of boxes at a fixed computational scale; resolution changes both evidence and computation. The component results therefore motivated a quality-oriented calibration rather than another hand adjustment of the original coefficients.

V1 replaced manually chosen controller parameters with validation-driven optimization. Its parameters were selected on the calibration data and then frozen as Trial 24 before the historical held-out evaluation. V1 should be understood as a quality-oriented profile: its purpose was to maximize the selected tracking quality objective under the historical protocol, not to establish a universal policy for every tracker. On the historical VisDrone comparison, the baseline obtained HOTA 28.430, MOTA 19.729, IDF1 32.724, IDS 1235, and 36.528 FPS. V1 obtained HOTA 33.835, MOTA 26.948, IDF1 41.546, IDS 1184, and 38.985 FPS. Its paired bootstrap changes versus baseline were $+5.4051$ HOTA [4.1273,6.9022], $+7.2195$ MOTA [5.4362,9.2934], and $+8.8220$ IDF1 [6.9005,11.0537]. IDS was not significant in that archived analysis. These values establish a historical quality profile with uncertainty, not a direct numerical comparison with our model.

V2 was created for a different reason. A system with a strong aggregate quality score may still produce identity fragmentation or excessive false positives, and a deployment may value a different quality--speed point. Trial 22 therefore explored a multi-objective MOTA/IDS profile. It obtained HOTA 31.218, MOTA 23.792, IDF1 37.870, IDS 919, and 46.024 FPS on the historical comparison. V2 reduced identity switches and increased speed relative to the historical baseline, but its HOTA was below V1. It is consequently a deliberate identity/false-positive/runtime trade-off, not a global replacement for the quality-oriented V1.

The historical UAVDT transfer tests the same frozen profiles without retuning. Baseline HOTA/MOTA/IDF1/IDS were 24.085/13.841/27.887/558; V1 obtained 28.390/17.399/34.453/321; V2 obtained 26.930/16.118/32.014/308. The transfer supports the portability of frozen operating profiles, while the different V1/V2 patterns reinforce that tracker quality, identity stability, and throughput are not reducible to one scalar objective.

The evolution therefore has a clear logic. Original AC--MOT established a lightweight five-cue controller. Ablation showed that detector controls affected downstream tracking. V1 calibrated the quality profile. V2 explored a different deployment trade-off. The modern redesign then tightened provenance, split development from evaluation, tested two tracker hosts, and added runtime and attribution controls because the historical results alone could not identify whether the improvement came from dynamic timing, operating-point calibration, tracker interaction, or some combination.

This chronology also prevents anachronistic interpretation of the final action map. The historical controller's 640/736/832 rule was a hand-designed scene-to-resolution mapping coupled to confidence and NMS formulas. V1 and V2 changed the selection problem rather than merely changing a threshold. Our modern model then used a different detector and action set. The fact that our model has LOW=1536 and HIGH=1088 is not an error inherited from the historical design; it is a frozen result of a modern calibration search whose action names describe state labels rather than resolution rank.
