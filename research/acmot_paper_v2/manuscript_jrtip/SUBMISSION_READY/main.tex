\abstract{
Unmanned-aerial-vehicle multi-object tracking must balance observation quality and latency while density, target scale, illumination, and blur vary within a video. We frame AC--MOT as an optimization-guided, training-free detector-control layer that changes confidence, non-maximum-suppression, and input resolution while preserving detector--tracker separation. V1 was the first formal quality-oriented Optuna calibration, and V2 explicitly optimized the identity--runtime trade-off through a feasible MOTA/IDS Pareto study. Historical transfer and cross-pipeline studies show that the resulting profiles are objective- and host-dependent. We then freeze a modern v3 policy with causal crowd-weighted scene control, recovery, hysteresis, dwell, two tracker hosts, paired bootstrap evidence, and end-to-end timing. On the documented second read of seven VisDrone2019-MOT sequences, AC--MOT v3 improves HOTA by 2.937 points with ByteTrack and 1.325 points with OATrack; corresponding MOTA changes are 17.995 and 6.318 points. Tesla T4 throughput is 13.45 and 15.27 frames/s, including approximately 4.3 and 4.2 ms controller overhead. A no-retuning UAVDT transfer is strongly positive for ByteTrack and mixed for OATrack. The evidence supports objective-driven detector operating-point control and observation-set shaping with tracker- and dataset-dependent outcomes, bounded by the modern frozen v3 validation protocol.
}

\keywords{multi-object tracking; UAV video; adaptive inference; real-time image processing}
