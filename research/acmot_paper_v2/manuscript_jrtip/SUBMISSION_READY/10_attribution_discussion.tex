\section{Attribution and discussion}
The experiments establish that detector operating point affects downstream MOT,
that v3 changes the declared high-resolution baseline on two tracker hosts, and
that operating-point choices shape measured compute. The remaining question is
whether the observed quality difference is caused specifically by the exact
scene-conditioned switching schedule.

The matched static control uses resolution 1088, NMS IoU 0.45, and detector
confidence 0.40. Its HOTA is approximately 44.530 for ByteTrack and 48.269 for
OATrack, close to v3 values 44.523 and 48.095. The comparison against
\texttt{r1536\_n70} therefore supports moving away from the declared baseline,
but does not isolate an additional accuracy benefit from changing the action
frame by frame.

The timing control has the same implication: development mean $\Delta$HOTA is
2.446957 for the causal schedule and 2.617228 for a same-action-distribution
shuffled schedule. This does not show that random timing is globally superior;
both are calibration statistics from one frozen control. It does show that the
present evidence cannot claim exact scene-conditioned timing as necessary for
the baseline-relative gain.

The controller nevertheless has systems value when much of the accuracy
difference is explained by operating-point mixture. It provides online compute
allocation, reacts to changing scene statistics, and exposes a finite action
interface without retraining detector weights. A dynamic policy may therefore
be preferable under an average detector-time budget even when a matched static
point has similar HOTA. This is a deployment interpretation, not a new causal
claim.

Tracker dependence supports keeping the hosts separate. ByteTrack starts from a
weaker modern baseline and shows a larger VisDrone change; OATrack starts
stronger and has a smaller HOTA increment and essentially flat UAVDT HOTA
transfer. The detector-side policy changes the observations available to each
association algorithm, so a pooled ``AC--MOT gain'' would be misleading.

The negative searches also bound the conclusion. Resolution-only, NMS-only,
cue-subset, and earlier joint formulations did not pass the predeclared
matched-static robustness gate. The final candidate was selected against
\texttt{r1536\_n70}, not because the archive established that scene timing beat
every fixed point. The selection audit records that Trial 7 was not the literal
argmax under either eligibility interpretation; the freeze is preserved and
the deviation is reported as provenance.

The strongest supported conclusion is that AC--MOT is an auditable,
training-free mechanism for shaping the detector observation stream and
computational operating point of aerial MOT. It can improve a declared baseline
and transfer with tracker-dependent outcomes. It does not yet establish that
exact causal switching adds a separate, consistent accuracy benefit beyond
operating-point calibration and action-mixture effects. Cost-matched randomized
policies, more detectors and trackers, and a predeclared dynamic-scheduling
objective are the appropriate future tests.
